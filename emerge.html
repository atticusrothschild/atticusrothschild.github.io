Undergraduate research · June 2026–present

EMERGE Lab

At Rice’s EMERGE Lab, I fabricate transistors in the cleanroom and design microscale mechanical devices for research into predicting device behavior.

Transistor fabrication

I’m responsible for fabricating transistors for a project exploring whether machine-learning models can predict the behavior of other transistors made from the same material. I make the devices; a collaborator performs the electrical testing and model training.

We’re starting with silicon, with plans to extend the work to two-dimensional materials and bioelectronic devices for neural interfaces. The planned bioelectronics work would explore whether measured device data can support predictive models for materials used to interface with the brain.

Cleanroom processes

My hands-on work includes spin-coating photoresists, baking, photolithography and development, chemical removal of polymer layers, and plasma cleaning.

I also use atomic layer deposition (ALD), ellipsometry, and wafer dicing as part of the fabrication workflow. These processes span thin-film deposition, film characterization, and separating wafers into individual chips.

MEMS microrelay

Alongside the transistor work, I’m designing a spring-based microrelay: a microelectromechanical switch that uses physical motion to open or close an electrical connection.

The research goal is to explore whether the same modeling approach can predict the relay’s behavior, extending the investigation from electronic transistors to mechanical switching devices. The relay is currently in design.

Laser testing & positioning

My earlier work focused on a micromanipulator and light-tight enclosure for laser absorption testing of two-dimensional-material chips. The setup supports repeatable sample positioning while controlling light entering the experiment.

I worked on the mechanical design in CAD and the positioning system, which uses a Raspberry Pi Pico, stepper motors, and encoders. This work connects precision motion hardware with optical measurements.
