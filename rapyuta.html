Simulation internship · Tokyo · 2023

Rapyuta Robotics

I worked on the Unreal Engine simulation for Rapyuta ASRS, an automated storage and retrieval system, during its pre-release development. My work connected CAD, warehouse layout logic, and testing with physical robots.

Picking-station simulation

I created a CAD model of the human picking station and integrated it into the simulation. In the ASRS workflow, robots bring storage bins to a station where an operator picks items.

I developed placement logic that used the relative positions and orientations of warehouse tiles to identify a U-shaped arrangement and determine where the station should be generated. This linked the station’s placement to the surrounding layout.

Adding the picking station made the simulated environment more representative of the physical system and supported testing software and hardware changes before trying them on the real equipment. It also provided a foundation for future simulations of robotic arms performing the picking tasks.

Testing against physical robots

I tested physical robots against the simulation and investigated why larger groups disconnected or moved slowly. I improved the internal interface used to start robot images and reduced message traffic to address suspected network congestion. Together, these changes helped scale simultaneous physical-robot operation to 300% of its original capacity.

Demonstrations

I conducted real-world and simulated robot demonstrations for prospective clients.

Continuing robotics at Rice

Rice University · MECH 450 · Fall 2026

I’ve continued exploring robotics at Rice through Algorithmic and AI-enabled Robotics (MECH 450), an upper-level course I’m taking as a sophomore with the professor’s permission. The course extends my work with robot simulation into the algorithms that plan a robot’s movement.

I led the C++ implementation of a random-tree motion planner for a seven-joint Franka Panda robot arm. The planner searches for a route around obstacles by sampling joint configurations, checking connections for collisions, and tracing the resulting path through the tree.

For this team project, my partner built the test environments and ran the trials. Our planner found collision-free paths in two simulated scenes where moving directly from start to goal would hit an obstacle.
